Even for a sentence as seemingly obvious as "The Riemann hypothesis is either true or false," so obvious that it hardly seems worth disputing, some people seriously asked back, "How can you be sure of that?" The people who raised this question are the main characters of this seminar: the intuitionistic logicians. This seminar compares classical logic and intuitionistic logic along two axes: natural deduction and algebraic structures. Together, we will see that the two systems are in fact separated by the presence or absence of a single rule, reductio ad absurdum, and that this one rule draws the line in exactly the same place in their algebraic structures as well. Since natural deduction is a tool we repeatedly encounter when reading and writing proofs in analysis, topology, and many other courses, we hope this seminar will serve as an opportunity to revisit that sense once again. prerequisite_label_en: "Prerequisite" prerequisite_en: "None" # Media and materials (shared) video_heading: "Video Link" video_url: "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JF2yZJ0xj6U" thumbnail_src: "/seminars/2026Fall/thumbnails/7_Classical logic vs Intuitionistic logic.png" thumbnail_alt: "Classical logic vs. Intuitionistic logic seminar thumbnail" materials_heading: "Presentation Material" materials: - label: "7_Classical logic vs Intuitionistic logic.pdf" url: "/seminars/2026Fall/slidedecks/7_Classical logic vs Intuitionistic logic.pdf" ---
