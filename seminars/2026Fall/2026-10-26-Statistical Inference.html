Yet we often do not think deeply about why we should estimate them this way or why these estimators are good. Even after obtaining an estimate from a sample, it is not easy to judge how reliable it is. Statistical inference provides answers to these questions on the basis of statistical reasoning. This seminar begins with convergence in probability and convergence in distribution, which form the foundations of statistical inference, and explores the principles and properties of maximum likelihood estimators. We then use Fisher information and the Cramér-Rao lower bound to understand the precision and theoretical limits of estimators and learn how to judge what makes an estimator good. We also study sufficiency and completeness, using the Rao-Blackwell and Lehmann-Scheffé theorems to reduce the variance of unbiased estimators and ultimately obtain (uniformly) minimum-variance unbiased estimators [(U)MVUEs].
